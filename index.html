04My story

Building, breaking and rebuilding

This isn’t the usual engineering tale. It’s a mix of failures that almost broke me, choices that raised eyebrows, and breakthroughs that reshaped my purpose. Each chapter adds up to who I’m becoming.

  1. 01Self-Discovery

    The Misfit Student (School Years → Early College)

    Struggled with academics, constantly shifting interests between music, sports, art, and tech tinkering. Explored coding (Python, C++, C, DSA, Data Science) but never stuck for long. Started a small photocopy/printing business in parallel.

    Learnt best by building, not memorizing

  2. 02Failure

    Business Experiment & The Great Fall (College Years)

    Scaled the photocopy/printing business and made steady profits. Shifted into stocks, F&O, and crypto trading. Overconfidence led to losses. Skipped exams and the business collapsed.

    100% financial loss, but gained humility & resilience

  3. 03Turning Point

    The IoT Spark

    A simple IoT plant watering project turned into a learning spree with ESP32, sensors, and I2C communication. Professor Ankit Jain kept pushing with new challenges.

    Discovered passion for building

    See the project
  4. 04Growth

    The Maker Obsession

    Dived into home automation, PCB design, CAD & 3D printing, Raspberry Pi, Jetson Nano, Linux, and AI projects like YOLO implementation.

    IoT & AI projects built

    Browse projects
  5. 05Failure

    Home Automation Startup

    Tried launching a home automation business with strong tech but lacked capital and team coordination. Business ended quickly.

    Lesson: Skills ≠ Business Success

  6. 06Growth

    AI on the Edge: Jetson Nano Experiments

    Ventured into edge AI using the Jetson Nano, experimenting with YOLO object detection, gesture recognition, and live camera inference. Logged every error, fix, and discovery to build a personal AI reference repository.

    From setting up CUDA environments to running real-time classification, these experiments made me confident integrating AI directly into hardware systems.

    From electronics to intelligence, learned to merge hardware and AI.

    Jetson Nano Hacks
  7. 07Milestone

    Fruit Freshness Project

    Built an integrated AI + IoT warehouse management system: freshness detection, OCR, brand recognition, sensors, and automated sorting.

    First end-to-end system integration

    See the project
  8. 08Milestone

    Reaching for the Stars

    Joined the Space Club under the guidance of Sandeep Khare Sir. Built robots, avionics, payloads, fiberglass rocket tubes, and composites. Led teams and taught members. Though we didn’t reach the final stages of competitions, every failure became a lesson, and we continue building for upcoming challenges.

    Learned rocketry hands-on through failures, teamwork, and persistence

    See the rocketry work
  9. 09Educator

    Becoming an Educator

    Conducted workshops on IoT, PCB design, avionics, and electronics. Taught hundreds of students, sparking curiosity and confidence.

    Teaching became a source of pride

  10. 10Learner

    Learner of Tools

    Gained hands-on experience with PCB plotter, CNC, laser engraver, bench power supply, and test equipment. Learned mechanics, precision, and control systems.

    Learned the tools that bring ideas to life.

  11. 11Milestone

    Teknofest & IN-SPACe / ISRO Insights

    Represented our college in two national-level aerospace challenges. At Teknofest (Turkey), we designed a complete rocket avionics and payload system, cleared every major review stage, and received official approval for final launch, but failed at the second-last stage due to coordination and submission delays.

    The failure was tough but transformative, it taught me that projects don’t fail because of bad circuits; they fail because of poor coordination. I learned how leadership, accountability, and clear communication matter more than technical perfection.

    In the following year, we applied those lessons at the IN-SPACe / ISRO National Challenge, competing in both Rocket and CanSat categories, securing 2nd Prize (Rocket Competition) and Best CanSat Design Award, 4th Position nationally.

    Turned failure into a framework, learned to lead, coordinate, and rebuild effectively.

    Read the insights
  12. 12Transition

    Still Building, Even When Paused

    The Narsimha Rocket Launch with Rocketeers was originally scheduled for 31st January 2026, but the competition was canceled before the final launch phase.

    Despite the cancellation, the system was fully prepared, including the avionics PCB that I designed with my team during the IN-SPACe / ISRO competition.

    Not every project reaches the sky, sometimes they pause before liftoff. But the learning, design, and effort remain just as real.

    Still building, even when the launch doesn’t happen.

  13. 13Professional Chapter

    Technical Trainer @ InUnity

    Joined InUnity, Mangalore on 5th January as a Technical Trainer. Teaching electronics, IoT, embedded systems, and practical engineering workflows to students while continuously refining my own understanding.

    This role represents a shift from building only for myself to enabling others to build with confidence.

    Learning scaled through teaching.

  14. 14Growth

    Promoted to Program Mentor

    Promoted to Program Mentor on 1st March at InUnity.

    Taught first-year B.Tech students about Design Thinking, Secondary Research, and Problem Analysis, along with hands-on exposure to various prototyping tools such as IoT, CAD, Vibe Coding, and 3D Printing.

    Concluded my journey with InUnity on 27th June, closing a chapter that taught me how to structure learning for others at scale.

    Turning ideas into real-world prototypes.

    See the program
  15. 15Professional Chapter

    Incubation Associate @ PSIT Kanpur

    Joined PSIT Kanpur on 1st July as an Incubation Associate, working with early-stage student startups, helping them move from raw ideas to working prototypes and structured ventures.

    A shift from teaching individual skills to shaping entire ventures, mentoring founders on technology, product validation, and execution.

    From building products to building builders.

  16. 16Milestone

    First Car: Toyota Glanza

    Bought my first car, a Toyota Glanza, a small milestone that marked a step forward after years of building, teaching, and juggling ventures.

    A reminder that progress isn’t only measured in projects and titles, but also in the personal wins along the way.

    A personal milestone alongside the professional journey.

  17. 17Founder

    Founding Falcon: The Drone Club

    Founded Falcon, a drone club at PSIT Kanpur, and currently serve as its Club Ambassador, building a community around UAV design, flight controllers, avionics, and autonomous systems.

    What started as a personal obsession with hardware has become a shared space where students learn to design, build, and fly their own machines.

    Building a community, not just a project.

  18. 18Founder

    Falcon Merges with PSIT Vyomnauts

    Founded Falcon, a drone club at PSIT Kanpur, initially running it solo as its Club Ambassador. Falcon has since merged with PSIT Vyomnauts, the college’s space club, bringing drones and rocketry under one shared community.

    The club now runs on the combined strength of both teams, carrying forward the experience and mentorship of Vyomnauts Ambassador Sandeep Khare, alongside UAV design, flight controllers, avionics, and autonomous systems work from Falcon.

    Two communities, one mission, building together instead of alone.

  19. 19Educator

    Conducting the Model Rocketry Workshop by IN-SPACe

    I had the opportunity to be part of a team selected by **IN-SPACe** as one of just **4 organisations** invited to conduct hands-on Model Rocketry workshops, alongside BAAS Technologies, Thrust Tech India Pvt. Ltd., and Synodic Space Labs Private Limited.

    Mentored an enthusiastic batch of students together with **Sandeep Kumar Khare** and **Ritish Katiyar**, taking them from a blank sheet of paper to a rocket ready for launch. The curriculum covered OpenRocket aerostructure design, EasyEDA PCB design, electronics fundamentals (digital, analog, I2C, SPI, UART), microcontroller programming for avionics, algorithm testing in a vacuum chamber, soldering and zero-PCB board building, parachute design and packing, and complete hands-on assembly of a model rocket designed for a 100-metre launch.

    Watching young minds build their first rocket, sharpen their engineering skills, design their own circuit boards, test their own code, and prepare a rocket for flight in a matter of days stands out as one of the most fulfilling experiences of my journey so far.

    From being a student fascinated by aerospace to mentoring the next generation of innovators, bringing practical aerospace engineering closer to young, ambitious minds has been an incredibly rewarding experience.

    From student to mentor, bringing real aerospace engineering to young, ambitious minds.

    See it on LinkedIn

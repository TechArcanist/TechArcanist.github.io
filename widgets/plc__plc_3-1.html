DC vs AC: Two Kinds of Electricity

Electrical devices run on one of two types of current: DC (direct current) or AC (alternating current). They behave very differently, and in a PLC panel you will meet both. We use the same picture as before: green charges travelling round a loop. Every step has a simulator: drag the sliders and watch what changes. All numbers on this page are teaching values.

+ WORDS USED ON THIS PAGE (OPEN THIS IF A WORD IS NEW)
DC (direct current)
Current that flows in one direction only, from + to −. The voltage stays constant.
AC (alternating current)
Current whose charges move back and forth. The voltage keeps rising, falling and swapping sides.
frequency (f), unit: hertz (Hz)
How many complete back-and-forth cycles happen in one second. Mains is 50 Hz or 60 Hz.
period (T), unit: second (s)
The time one cycle takes. T = 1 ÷ f.
waveform
A graph of voltage against time.
peak and RMS
Peak is the top of the AC wave. RMS is the value printed on real devices: peak ÷ 1.414.
control panel
The metal cabinet holding the PLC, power supplies, relays and wiring that automate a machine.
HMI
Human-machine interface: the operator screen.
contactor
A heavy-duty switch that a small control signal closes to power a motor.
single phase
One AC wave, like the one in this lesson. Three phase (three waves) comes in a later lesson.

Key idea. DC controls, AC powers. If you want a motor to have enough power, supply it with AC. If you want to switch that motor on and off, control it with DC.

PART 3 Worked examples

Read the question, work it out on paper, then press Show answer.

Example 1: A 50 Hz supply. How long does one cycle take?

T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 s = 20 ms

Example 2: One cycle takes 1/60 of a second. What is the frequency?

f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ (1/60) = 60 Hz

Example 3: How many back-and-forth cycles happen in one minute on a 50 Hz supply?

50 × 60 = 3000 cycles

Example 4: A PLC and an HMI sit inside a control panel. Do they run on AC or DC, and at what voltage?

DC, usually 24 V DC (some devices use 12 V DC).

Example 5: An AC wave has a peak of 220 V. What is its lowest value?

−220 V

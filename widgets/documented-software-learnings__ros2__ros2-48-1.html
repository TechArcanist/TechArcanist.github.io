Chapter 48: static and dynamic transformations

Fixed or Moving? Static and Dynamic Frames, /tf_static and /tf

The frames of a robot come in two kinds. Static frames never move compared with the robot, like a camera held by screws. Dynamic frames do move, like a wheel that turns. The two kinds travel on two different topics, /tf_static and /tf, and RViz reads both to draw the robot. Every lesson has something to move and watch. The numbers are only examples, and the pictures are drawn from the side to keep them easy to read.

Words used on this page (open this if a word is new)
static frameA frame that never moves compared with the frame it is linked to. Examples are a camera or a lidar screwed to the body of the robot.
dynamic frameA frame that can move compared with the frame it is linked to, so its transform changes over time. An example is a turning wheel.
fixed jointA joint in the URDF that holds two links in place. It is used for static frames.
continuous jointA joint in the URDF that can turn without limit, like the axle of a wheel. It is used for dynamic frames.
URDFThe file that describes the model of the robot: its links and how they are connected by joints.
robot_state_publisherThe node that reads the URDF and the joint angles, and publishes the transforms of all the frames of the robot.
/tf_staticThe topic where the transforms of fixed joints are published, once.
/tfThe topic where the transforms that change over time are published, again and again.
frequency (Hz)How many times per second something happens. 5 Hz means 5 times every second.
RVizA program that draws the robot and its frames in 3D from the data on the topics.
joint_state_publisher_guiA small window with sliders that gives the angles of the moving joints, such as the wheels.
base_footprint (frame B)The robot frame on the ground at the centre of the robot. On this page it is called B.
broadcasterA node that publishes transforms. A static broadcaster sends once. A dynamic broadcaster sends again and again.
kept messageThe topic /tf_static keeps its message, so a node that starts later still receives it. ROS 2 does this with a setting called transient local.

Static or dynamic: does the link move?

Every frame of a robot is linked to another frame. Some links never move, like a camera held by screws. Other links move, like the link to a turning wheel. The first kind is static and the second kind is dynamic. Press Next and Back to move between steps, and use the sliders and Play.

Key idea. Static frames define the architecture of the robot and its sensors. Their links are mechanical, like parts screwed together, so they never change. Dynamic frames, like the wheels, change over time.
02

Two topics: /tf_static and /tf

The node robot_state_publisher reads the model of the robot and sends the transforms out on topics. The fixed ones go out once and the moving ones go out again and again. Press Next and Back to move between steps, and use the sliders and Play.

Key idea. robot_state_publisher reads the URDF. The transforms of fixed joints are published only once, on /tf_static. The transforms of moving joints, like wheels, change over time, so they are published again and again, at a specific frequency, on /tf.
03

RViz uses both topics

Other nodes can read the two topics. RViz is one of them. It uses the data to draw the robot and to show how its moving parts, like wheels, turn. Press Next and Back to move between steps, and use the switches, sliders and Play.

Key idea. Other nodes, like RViz, use the information on /tf_static and /tf. For example, RViz draws the static robot model and also shows how the dynamic parts move, like the rotation of the wheels.

05

Summary and quiz

Key takeaways

  1. The frames of a robot, and of a robotics application in general, can be of two types: static or dynamic.
  2. Static frames define the architecture of the robot and its sensors. The links between them are mechanical, like a camera screwed to the main frame of the robot. The camera link is fixed and cannot move compared with the robot frame.
  3. Static links are defined in the URDF model with fixed joints.
  4. The node robot_state_publisher reads the URDF, interprets it and publishes the transforms. The transforms of fixed joints are published only once, on the topic /tf_static.
  5. Dynamic frames, like the wheels, can move compared with the robot. The transform that says how a wheel is connected and oriented changes over time, so it is published again and again, at a specific frequency, on the topic /tf.
  6. Other nodes, like RViz, use the information on /tf and /tf_static to show the static robot model and how its dynamic parts move, for example how the wheels rotate.
  7. In the next lab lesson, a node publishes a static transform on /tf_static, and then a dynamic transform that varies over time on /tf.

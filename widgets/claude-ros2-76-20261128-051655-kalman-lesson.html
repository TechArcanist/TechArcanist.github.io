Chapter 76: the Kalman filter

Kalman Filter A Bayes filter with Gaussians: readings make the bell narrower, moving makes it wider

Chapters 72 to 75 gave the robot several sensors and their noise. The Kalman filter is the algorithm that fuses their data and filters the noise. This page follows a robot on a straight road: a first guess, a rock detector that sharpens it, a move that blurs it again, and the loop that repeats these steps.

Words used on this page (open this if a word is new)
Kalman filterA Bayes filter that assumes Gaussian noise for the motion and the sensor.
Bayes filterAn algorithm that updates a belief with Bayes rule when new evidence arrives.
prior beliefThe estimate of the position before a new reading or move.
measurement updateThe estimate of the position from a sensor reading, a Gaussian.
posteriorThe new belief after combining two pieces of information.
state predictionPredicting the next position after the robot moved.
μ and σThe centre (best guess) and the spread (uncertainty) of a Gaussian, here in metres.

Summary and quiz

Key takeaways

  1. The Kalman filter fuses sensor data and filters noise. It is a Bayes filter that assumes Gaussian noise.
  2. The prior belief is a first, uncertain estimate of the position.
  3. A sensor reading gives the measurement update; combining it with the prior gives a posterior narrower than both.
  4. The posterior becomes the prior of the next step.
  5. Moving the robot (state prediction) adds the motion error, so the posterior gets wider.
  6. The loop: initial estimate, measurement update, state prediction, again and again.

Introduction to the pose of a robot

Where is the robot, and which way does it face?

Before the equations that link wheel speeds and robot motion, one question needs a clear answer: how can the pose of a mobile robot on a flat floor be written in a mathematical form? This page gives the first picture of it: a robot on the x y plane, a fixed frame W, a moving frame R, three numbers (x, y, θ), and the split of a pose into a translation and a rotation. The equations themselves come in the next chapter. All numbers on the page are teaching values.

What is the pose of a mobile robot?

Before the speed of the wheels is connected to the motion of the robot, one thing comes first: how to say in a mathematical form where a mobile robot is. The robot can be simplified as an object that is restricted to move on a two dimensional plane. With a top down view, it only moves on the x y plane. The question is then: what is the pose of the robot? Move it below and see.

Key idea. The robot is simplified as an object moving on the x y plane. Its pose says where it is and which way it faces.
02

Two reference frames: W and R

The problem of the pose becomes the problem of giving the position and orientation of an object with respect to a fixed reference frame. So a fixed frame called W, the world, is defined. To make the idea simpler, a second frame is added: a mobile frame R that is attached to the robot and moves as the robot moves. The pose of the robot is now the position and orientation of frame R with respect to frame W.

Key idea. W is fixed and R moves with the robot. The pose of the robot is the pose of R with respect to W.
03

Three variables: x, y and theta

Now it is easy to see that three variables define the pose of frame R with respect to frame W. Two of them, x and y, are the coordinates of the centre of R measured from the centre of W. The third, the angle θ (theta), is the orientation of R with respect to W. Since R is attached to the robot, these same three numbers are the pose of the robot as it moves in the plane.

Key idea. Pose = (x, y, θ). Three numbers are enough for a robot on a plane.
04

Translation and rotation

The problem can be broken into two smaller ones. A pose is the combination of two elementary parts of motion: the translation, which gives the position (x, y), and the rotation, which gives the orientation θ. They will be studied one by one in the next chapter. After that the results are combined into the more general roto-translation motion. Here is a first look.

Key idea. Pose = translation (x, y) + rotation (θ). Together they make a roto-translation.
05

Summary and quiz

Key takeaways

  1. The mobile robot is simplified as an object on the x y plane, seen from above.
  2. Its pose is the position and the orientation of the robot.
  3. W is a fixed world frame. R is a frame attached to the robot that moves with it.
  4. The pose of the robot is the pose of R with respect to W.
  5. Three variables are enough: x, y and the angle θ.
  6. A pose is a translation (x, y) and a rotation (θ). Together they are a roto-translation.

Chapter 79: the extended Kalman filter

Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) Why the Kalman filter needs straight lines and bell curves, and how the extended Kalman filter gets them back

Chapter 78 showed the Kalman filter at work: it fused the noisy wheels and the IMU into a better angular velocity. That filter rests on two assumptions that the real world often breaks. This page shows the two assumptions, what a curved model does to a bell curve, and the one extra step, linearization, that turns the Kalman filter into the extended Kalman filter used in robotics.

Words used on this page (open this if a word is new)

modelA rule that turns one number into another, for example how a motion changes the state, or what a sensor should read.
linearShaped like a straight line: equal input steps give equal output changes.
non-linearShaped like a curve: equal input steps give different output changes.
GaussianThe bell curve, described by its mean (the middle) and its variance (how spread it is).
linearizationReplacing a curve by the straight line that fits it best at one point.
extended Kalman filter (EKF)The Kalman filter with a linearization step, so it can handle curved models.

The two assumptions of the Kalman filter

The Kalman filter is one member of the family of Bayes filters. It only works under two assumptions. Use Next and Back, the sliders and the buttons. Point at a coloured word to light up its part of the picture.

Key idea. The Kalman filter assumes that the motion model and the measurement model are linear (a straight line), and that the belief is a Gaussian, so it only has to estimate two numbers: the mean and the variance. In the real world these two assumptions are often not valid.

02

Straight lines and curves

A model is a rule that turns one number into another. Some rules are straight lines, some are curves. Use Next and Back, the sliders and the buttons. Point at a coloured word to light up its part of the picture.

Key idea. A linear model is a straight line: every equal step of the input changes the output by the same amount. A non-linear model is a curve: equal steps change the output by different amounts. Many real robot models are curves.

03

A bell curve through a line and through a curve

The motion model and the measurement model act on the belief, which is a bell curve. What comes out depends on whether the model is a straight line or a curve. Use Next and Back, the sliders and the buttons. Point at a coloured word to light up its part of the picture.

Key idea. A straight-line model turns a Gaussian into another Gaussian, with a different mean and variance that can be calculated. A curved model turns it into a shape that is not a Gaussian, so its mean and variance can no longer be calculated the Kalman way.

04

Linearization: the extended Kalman filter

The extended Kalman filter adds one step to the Kalman loop: it replaces the curve with the straight line that fits it best at one point. Use Next and Back, the sliders and the buttons. Point at a coloured word to light up its part of the picture.

Key idea. Linearization finds the linear function that best approximates the non-linear model at a specific point of the curve. With that straight line the belief stays a Gaussian. The extended Kalman filter is the Kalman loop with this step: initial estimate, linearization, then measurement update and state prediction.

Summary and quiz

Key takeaways

  1. The Kalman filter is a Bayes filter that assumes straight-line (linear) motion and measurement models and a Gaussian belief.
  2. With a Gaussian belief the filter only estimates two numbers, the mean and the variance.
  3. A straight-line model turns a Gaussian into another Gaussian with a new mean and variance.
  4. A curved (non-linear) model turns a Gaussian into a lopsided shape that is not a Gaussian, so the Kalman maths no longer fits.
  5. Linearization replaces the curve by the straight line that fits it best at one point. Near that point the two almost match.
  6. The extended Kalman filter is the Kalman loop plus linearization: initial estimate, linearization, measurement update, state prediction.

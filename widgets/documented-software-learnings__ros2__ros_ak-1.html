start here if maths feels rustyThe maths in plain English

Five small lessons. Each one answers “why are we doing this?” in everyday words.

Lesson 1. What is an angle?

An angle says how far you have turned. Face east: that is 0. Turn a quarter of the way round to the left and you face north: that is 90°. A full circle is 360°.

Computers and ROS prefer radians. It is just another unit for the same turn: a full circle is 2π ≈ 6.28 radians, a half turn is π ≈ 3.14, a quarter turn is 1.57. Convert with radians = degrees × π / 180.

Lesson 2. Why subtract the angles?

Both turtles measure their direction from the same place: the world’s east. But what we want is how much Turtle 2 is turned compared with Turtle 1.

You face 30°. Your friend faces 75°. How far must you turn to face the same way as your friend? 75 − 30 = 45°. That is all θ = θ2 − θ1 means.

positive = turned left, negative = turned right

cos and sin: the two-second version

cos θ = how much of one step goes sideways. sin θ = how much goes up. If you know SOH-CAH-TOA, it is the same thing with the long side equal to 1.

0°: all sideways. 90°: all up. 45°: equal parts (0.707 each).

What is a matrix?

Just a small table of numbers. The rotation matrix is a recipe card that says “to turn something by θ, mix its x and y like this”. Using it is one rule: multiply matching pairs, then add.

R = [ cos θ  −sin θ ;  sin θ  cos θ ]

why bother?Where robots use this

Moving in the direction you face

A robot driving at speed v while facing θ moves sideways by v·cos θ and up by v·sin θ every second. In code: x += v*cos(theta)*dt. This is exactly how the Turtlesim turtle moves.

Switching viewpoints (tf2)

A laser sees an obstacle “2 m ahead” of the robot. To put it on the world map you must turn that view by the robot’s angle. That turning is multiplication by R.

Robot arms

Every joint of an arm is a rotation. Chaining joints means multiplying their rotation matrices, which gives the position of the hand.

Steering to a goal

“How far must I turn to face the goal?” is a difference of angles, θ = goal angle − my angle. The same subtraction as 75 − 30 = 45.

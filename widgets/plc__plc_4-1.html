Single Phase, Three Phase and AC Around the World

In the last lesson we learned about AC. Now we answer two simple questions. First: what is the difference between single phase and three phase? Second: is the AC supply the same in every country? phase line (L)
A live wire that carries one AC voltage wave. Three phase has L1, L2 and L3.
neutral (N)
The return line.
single phase
One phase line and one neutral line. Used in homes.
three phase
Three phase lines, each with its own wave. Used in industry for big motors.
phase difference
The gap in time between the moments the three lines reach their peak.
phase-to-phase voltage
The voltage measured between any two phase lines.
phase-to-neutral voltage
The voltage measured between any phase line and the neutral.
3 wires and 4 wires
3 wires = L1, L2, L3 only (no neutral). 4 wires = L1, L2, L3 and N.
frequency (Hz)
How many AC cycles happen in one second: 50 Hz or 60 Hz.

PART 2 Questions beginners ask

Where is single phase used, and where is three phase used? Homes use single phase for lamps, sockets and small motors such as a household water pump. Industry uses three phase for big motors, large pumps and machines. These are the machines a PLC controls.

What is the neutral for? It is the return wire for single-phase loads such as lamps and sockets, and it gives them the smaller voltage (230 V instead of 400 V). A three-phase motor does not need it. Step 6 shows this.

Is the neutral the same as the minus (−) in DC? Almost. It is the return wire. In AC the current goes out along the phase line, then comes back along the neutral, and then the direction flips, many times every second.

Some supplies have no neutral. How does the current return? In a 3-wire supply the three phases share the job: current that goes out along one comes back along the others. You do not need to work this out now. Just remember that a three-phase motor needs only L1, L2 and L3.

Why two voltage ratings? Because there are two ways to connect equipment. A three-phase motor is connected to the phases, so it uses the phase-to-phase voltage (like 380 V or 400 V). A lamp is connected between one phase and the neutral, so it uses the smaller phase-to-neutral voltage (like 220 V or 230 V). The motor nameplate tells you which one it needs.

PART 5 Worked examples

Read the question, think about it, then press Show answer.

Example 1: Which wires does a 3-wire three-phase supply have?

L1, L2 and L3. There is no neutral.

Example 2: The country list shows 4 in the Wires column. What does it mean?

Three phase with neutral: L1, L2, L3 and N.

Example 3: A 380/220 V system. What does a voltmeter read between L1 and L2? Between L1 and N?

L1 to L2 (two phases): 380 V
L1 to N (phase and neutral): 220 V

Example 4: Is a lamp in a house single phase or three phase?

Single phase: it connects between one phase and the neutral.

Example 5: Your machine is rated 400 V, 50 Hz. Will it match Canada?

No. Canada lists 120/208 V, 240 V, 480 V and 347/600 V at 60 Hz, so neither the voltage nor the frequency matches.

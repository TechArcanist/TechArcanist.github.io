Differential kinematics

From the wheel speeds to the motion of the robot

All the tools are now available to relate the rotational speed of the robot wheels to the velocity of the robot, both linear and angular, in the robot frame. These relations make it possible to send velocity commands in the robot frame, for example straight ahead at 1 metre per second, and the wheels are driven accordingly. This is essential to move the robot with a joystick, which comes in the last part of this section. The velocity of the robot in the world frame is found with the rotation matrix. Joining the two gives the differential kinematics, the main topic here: what it is, why it is needed and what it is used for. Each step below has a simulator. All numbers on the page are teaching values.

Words used on this page (open this if a word is new)
velocityHow fast something moves and in which direction. Metres per second for moving, radians per second for turning.
linear velocity vHow fast the robot moves forward, in metres per second (m/s).
angular velocity ω (omega)How fast the robot turns, in radians per second (rad/s). Counter-clockwise is positive.
wheel speedHow fast a wheel turns, in radians per second.
robot frame RThe frame attached to the robot. Its x axis points straight ahead. It moves and turns with the robot.
world frame WThe fixed frame of the map. It never moves.
velocity commandA wish given to the robot in its own frame, such as: go straight at 1 m/s.
rotation matrixThe tool from the earlier chapters that turns something from the robot frame R to the world frame W.
differential kinematicsThe link between the speed of the wheels and the velocity of the robot, in the robot frame and in the world frame. It is also used the other way round, to turn a wanted motion into wheel speeds.

01

What is differential kinematics?

Differential kinematics is the main topic of this chapter. It is the link between the speed of the wheels of a robot and the velocity of the robot. Choose a question below: what it is, why it is needed, and what it is used for.

Key idea. Differential kinematics links the wheel speeds with the velocity of the robot. Used the other way round, it turns a wanted motion into wheel speeds, which is what a joystick needs.
02

From the wheel speeds to the velocity of the robot

The rotational speed of the two wheels decides the velocity of the robot in its own frame R: the linear velocity v, how fast it moves forward, and the angular velocity ω, how fast it turns. Change the wheel speeds and watch both follow.

Key idea. The wheel speeds decide the velocity of the robot in its own frame: forward speed v and turning speed ω.
03

Sending velocity commands in the robot frame

The same link works the other way round. A velocity command is given directly in the robot frame, for example straight ahead at 1 metre per second, and the wheels are driven accordingly to give the desired speed in the desired direction. This is essential to move the robot with a joystick, which is done in the last part of this section.

Key idea. A command in the robot frame is turned into wheel speeds, so the robot does what was asked.
04

The velocity in the world frame, and the whole chain

Once the robot velocity in R is known, the velocity of the robot in the world frame W can be calculated too, with the rotation matrix, as was done before for positions. Combining the relation from the wheels to the robot velocity with this one gives the equation of the differential kinematics.

Key idea. Wheel speeds give the velocity in R. The rotation matrix turns it into the velocity in W. Together: differential kinematics.
05

Summary and quiz

Key takeaways

  1. The speeds of the two wheels give the velocity of the robot in its own frame: linear velocity and angular velocity.
  2. A velocity command can be sent in the robot frame, for example straight at 1 m/s, and the wheels are driven accordingly. This is essential for a joystick.
  3. The velocity in the world frame is found with the rotation matrix.
  4. Joining the two gives the differential kinematics.

The key chain

wheel speeds→velocity in R→velocity in W

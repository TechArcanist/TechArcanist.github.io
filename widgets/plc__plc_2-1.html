Voltage Drop, Current, Resistance and Power

In the last lesson we met voltage, current and resistance with a water tank. Now we use a different picture: charges (the green balls) travelling round a closed loop. We will see what a voltage drop is, how resistance controls current, and then learn a new quantity called power. Every step has a simulator: drag the sliders and watch what changes. All numbers on this page are teaching values.

+ WORDS USED ON THIS PAGE (OPEN THIS IF A WORD IS NEW)
charge
A tiny amount of electricity that moves round the circuit. We draw it as green balls.
voltage source, unit: volt (V)
A battery or power supply that pushes the charges.
resistor, unit: ohm (Ω)
A part that makes it harder for charges to flow.
voltage drop
The energy each charge gives up as it passes through a part.
current (I), unit: amp (A)
How many charges pass a point every second.
power (P), unit: watt (W)
How much energy is used every second.
power supply
A device that converts mains power into a steady voltage, such as 24 V DC for a control panel.
rating
The output voltage and current a supply is built to give.

PART 2 The four quantities

Keep this cheat-sheet in your head. The first three you already know. Power is new.

QuantitySymbolUnitFormula
VoltageVvolt (V)V = I × R
CurrentIampere (A)I = V ÷ R
ResistanceRohm (Ω)R = V ÷ I
PowerPwatt (W)P = V × I
Power rule. Less resistance, more voltage and more current all mean more power.

PART 3 Worked examples

Read the question, work it out on paper, then press Show answer.

Example 1: A DC power supply has an output current of 5 A and an output voltage of 24 V. What power can this device supply?

P = V × I = 24 × 5 = 120 W

Example 2: A 5 V source drives a 100 Ω resistor. Find the current, then the power.

I = V ÷ R = 5 ÷ 100 = 0.05 A
P = V × I = 5 × 0.05 = 0.25 W

Example 3: A phone charger is rated 5 V and 2 A. How much power can it supply?

P = 5 × 2 = 10 W

Example 4: What is the unit of power?

The watt (W). 1 W = 1 V × 1 A.
